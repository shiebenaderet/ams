New England

Massachusetts · New Hampshire · Rhode Island · Connecticut · Port: Boston

Farms in New England cannot produce crops such as tobacco, sugar cane, or rice: the soil is thin and rocky, and the growing season is short. Farmers raise some wheat, but barely enough for the region's own people. Instead, New Englanders turned to the sea. Fishing fleets work the North Atlantic and bring home far more cod than the colonies can eat, dried in the sun or salted to store.

Forests supply lumber for shipbuilding, and the ports of Boston (Massachusetts) and Newport (Rhode Island) are busy with trade. Your biggest advantage is your fleet: 7 merchant ships, the most of any colony. The Southern Colonies and the West Indies own no ships, so you can earn goods by carrying their cargo, for a price.

Rhode Island's turn into rum. Since you have no molasses, you must get it from the West Indies. The game's rule: acquire 2 molasses before you trade each 1 rum. That dependence is your biggest challenge. When the Molasses Act of 1733 taxed molasses from French islands, many New England merchants simply smuggled it.

New England's trade was tied to slavery. The molasses came from plantations worked by Africans, and the cheapest dried fish was shipped south to feed them. Newport merchants sent rum to West Africa to buy captive people, who were then carried across the Atlantic in the : weeks chained in crowded holds, where many died. Slavery was also legal in New England itself.

You have: fish, whale oil, lumber, rum, and 7 ships.

Words to know

Distillery
A factory that makes alcoholic drinks. New England distilleries made rum from West Indian molasses.
Molasses
A dark brown syrup left over when sugar is made. It sold for less than sugar, and rum is made from it.
Enslaved / Slavery
Slavery is a system in which people are held as property and forced to work without pay for life, with their children born into the same condition. Colonial laws tied slavery to African descent. Historians write enslaved people to keep their humanity in view.
The Middle Passage
The second leg of the triangular trade: the forced voyage of captive Africans across the Atlantic to the Americas, lasting weeks or months. Captives were chained in crowded holds, and roughly one in seven died on the way. Survivors were sold into slavery in the West Indies and the mainland colonies.
Export / Import
Exporting is selling goods abroad; importing is buying them from abroad. Every region must do both to win.
Raw Materials
Natural resources, such as lumber, fish, tobacco, and furs, before they are made into finished products.
Naval Stores
Products of pine forests (tar, pitch, and resin) essential for building and maintaining wooden ships.

Middle Colonies

New York · New Jersey · Pennsylvania · Delaware · Port: Philadelphia

Farms in the Middle Colonies produce large amounts of wheat and corn, much more than the colonists can use. That is why the region was called the breadbasket of British America. Some tobacco grows in Delaware, but not enough to . Pennsylvania and New York have plenty of timber, grain, and cattle to sell. Trade with Native American nations brings in furs, which sell for a high price in England.

Your main ports are Philadelphia (Pennsylvania) and New York City (New York). You own 2 merchant ships. That is enough to trade on your own, but small next to New England's 7 or England's 8.

Your biggest advantage is that food is a necessity, and you grow it. Your biggest challenge is that food is bulky and worth little per shipload. Sugar, by contrast, is expensive in England. Trade some of your products in the West Indies for sugar, then take that sugar to England for manufactured goods. The profitable route is often indirect.

That route depended on slavery. Sugar planters kept nearly every acre in cane and bought their food. Philadelphia flour and New York grain fed the Africans who worked the plantations, and colonial lumber became their barrels and buildings. Those Africans had survived the , the forced voyage across the Atlantic in which captives were chained below deck for weeks. Slavery also existed in New York, New Jersey, and Pennsylvania. New York City had one of the largest enslaved populations of any colonial city.

You have: wheat and grains, lumber, cattle, furs, and 2 ships.

Southern Colonies

Maryland · Virginia · N. Carolina · S. Carolina · Georgia · Port: Charleston

Tobacco is the major of the Southern Colonies. You grow far more than England can buy, and taxes plus oversupply have pushed the price down to about a penny a pound, though buyers elsewhere in Europe would pay more. Under the Navigation Act of 1660, tobacco could be sold only to England. South Carolina produces rice, some silk, and , the blue dye England wants for its cloth. North Carolina produces (pine tar and resin for shipbuilding). Maryland has a fishing industry, and furs come from trade with Native Americans.

Eight kinds of goods is the most varied supply in the game, and that is your biggest advantage. Your biggest challenge is that you have no merchant ships. Your ports, Charleston (South Carolina) and Savannah (Georgia), depend on others. You must hire a ship from New England, the Middle Colonies, or England, or trade for a ship of your own.

Every one of these crops rested on slavery. Planters forced Africans and their descendants to clear the land and grow the tobacco, rice, and indigo, without pay and for life. By the early 1700s, enslaved people were the majority of South Carolina's population. Many had survived the , the forced voyage across the Atlantic in chained, crowded holds, and more captives arrived at Charleston than at any other mainland port.

You have: tobacco, rice, naval stores, fish, lumber, furs, silk, indigo. No ships.

A blue dye made from plants, grown in South Carolina by enslaved workers; England wanted all of it for its cloth.

Products of pine forests (tar, pitch, and resin) essential for building and maintaining wooden ships.

West Indies

Jamaica · Barbados · Antigua · St. Kitts · Port: Kingston

The islands of the West Indies have large sugar cane plantations. Sugar is expensive and in demand all over the world, which makes it your biggest advantage. Making crystallized sugar also produces , a dark brown syrup that New England's distilleries depend on for rum. You also supply citrus fruit, which sailors ate to stay healthy on long voyages. Your ports include Kingston (Jamaica) and Bridgetown (Barbados).

Sugar required an enormous workforce, and planters filled it through the transatlantic . Africans were forcibly taken from their homes and carried through the Middle Passage, the brutal voyage across the Atlantic, then made to work the cane fields and boiling houses. By the mid-1700s, about nine in ten people in Jamaica were enslaved. The wealth of these islands cannot be separated from the people who were forced to produce it.

Your biggest challenge is that the islands grow almost nothing but sugar, so they must import food and manufactured goods. You own no ships and must hire one from New England, the Middle Colonies, or England, or trade for a ship. French, Spanish, and Dutch islands grow sugar too and often sell it cheaper; the Molasses Act of 1733 taxed their molasses to protect your trade.

You have: sugar, molasses, citrus fruit. No ships.

England

The Mother Country · Port: London

You represent the merchants of England, the . Under , colonies existed to benefit the nation that ruled them. They supplied raw materials and bought its . England controls most of the manufactured goods the colonies want: guns, gunpowder, clothing, furniture, tools, and books. The Navigation Acts, beginning in 1651, required colonial trade to travel on English ships. Many colonial goods could be sold only to England.

Your biggest advantages are that and your fleet. You have 8 ships, the most in the game, so you can also earn by carrying cargo for regions without ships. Parliament can place (taxes on imported goods) on trade; in the game, duties from chance cards are usually paid to England.

Your biggest challenge is your needs list, the longest in the game. Nearly everything on it is a colonial raw material: forest products for the fleet, cash crops for English buyers, and food. You depend on the colonies as much as they depend on you.

English ships were also the largest carriers of the transatlantic in the 1700s. They carried cloth, guns, and metal goods to West Africa, where they were traded for captive Africans. The captives were carried through the Middle Passage, the forced voyage across the Atlantic, and sold into slavery in the Americas. The ships returned with sugar and tobacco grown by people. Liverpool and Bristol grew rich from this trade.

You have: guns, gunpowder, clothing, fine furniture, tools, books, silver, and 8 ships.
