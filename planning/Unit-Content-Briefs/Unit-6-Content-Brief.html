Driving question

Why did the nation tear itself apart, and what did Reconstruction try to rebuild?

Supporting essential questions

  • Can you explain the Civil War without following the money? What do cotton, tariffs, and enslaved labor add to the story that “North vs. South” leaves out? (causation with economic evidence — E4.6-8.5, E2.6-8.5)
  • What did Reconstruction try to rebuild — and what did the nation leave undone? (ideals vs. reality — C1.6-8.4)
  • Who counts as a citizen, and what does it take to make rights on paper real in daily life? (feeds the Civic Action citizenship/rights lens in Unit 8)
  • How did individuals and movements — Frederick Douglass, Harriet Tubman, formerly enslaved people claiming freedom — force the nation to change? (H2.6-8.6)

Signature project — “The Breaking Point” causation analysis

Student-facing overview

Wars don't just happen — they're caused, and the causes are almost never as simple as “two sides disagreed.” In this project, you'll build your own evidence-backed claim about why the Civil War happened, and you'll have to support it three ways: with political evidence (laws, compromises, elections), social evidence (abolition, resistance, the lives of enslaved people), and economic evidence. That last one is where you'll think like an economist: we'll treat the cotton economy and enslaved labor as a supply-and-demand system — who profited, who paid, and why the people making money fought so hard to keep the system going. Then you'll follow the story past the war itself and weigh what Reconstruction actually rebuilt. Your final claim has to survive the toughest question in history class: “What's your evidence?”

Teacher notes: this is the year's capstone for multi-causal argument. Rubric outcomes to attach in Canvas: ★ H3.6-8.5 (analyze multiple causal factors), ★ E4.6-8.5 (economic evidence in the argument), and ★ H2.6-8.6 (individuals & movements shape history).
