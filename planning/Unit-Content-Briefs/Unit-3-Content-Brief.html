Driving question

How do you build a government that limits its own power — and who decides what the Constitution means?

Supporting essential questions

  • Why did America's first government fail — and what does that failure teach us about power?
  • How do checks and balances actually work when the branches collide in a real case?
  • Who did “We the People” include in 1787 — and who was written out?
  • When should one person's rights outweigh the common good? (the guiding thread of the Civics CBA)

Signature project — the SCOTUS Debate (Civics CBA)

Student-facing overview

By December, you'll know how the Constitution was built — the failed first try, the deals, the branches, the Bill of Rights. Then you'll do what the country has done ever since: argue about what it means. You'll take on a real landmark Supreme Court case — a student who wore an armband to school, a family whose door the police came through, a cheerleader who posted something she regretted — and argue one side in a formal debate: opening statement, cited evidence from your case's evidence vault, closing statement, works-cited page. This is your official Washington State Civics CBA, and it comes down to the same question the Court faces every term: where does one person's rights end and everyone else's good begin — and who gets to decide?

Teacher notes: the CBA is delivered through the mature scotus.mrbsocialstudies.org site — 7 cases (Tinker, Brown, T.L.O., Payton, Mahanoy, Kennedy, Arizona), evidence vaults, and the debate builder. Rubric outcomes to attach in Canvas: ★ C2.6-8.5 (checks & balances through a real case), ★ C4.6-8.4 (individual rights vs. common good), ★ SSS4.6-8.1 (thesis/product). Also served this unit: C1.6-8.3, C2.6-8.3, C2.6-8.6, SSS3.6-8.1, SSS4.6-8.2. See the alignment page.

Key vocabulary — 20 terms, 5 categories

The longest unit of the year gets the biggest vocab list. The five categories walk the unit's arc — from the Articles' failure, through the Convention's deals and the Constitution's machinery, to the Court that decides what it all means — and they're ordered for the site's mastery gating with scheduled unlocks (Oct 26 / Nov 9 / Nov 23 / Dec 7 / Jan 4). The “We the People?” category is the fix for the old constitutionreview site's imbalance: its “Inclusion and Exclusion” topic had 10 quiz questions but only 2 generic terms — it now carries 3 real, teachable terms. Tiers follow the studytools convention: must-know tested & central · encounter should recognize · bonus stretch.
