Unit 8 — Reform & Civic Action

← Course Overview · Outcomes Alignment · config.json draft
Weeks 36–40 · May 24 – Jun 22, 2027 (last day, Jun 22, is a half day — Civic Action products due) · Era: 1890–today · Theme color mulberry #7C2A48 (Unit Theme System)

Driving question

How have everyday people made change — and how will you?

Supporting essential questions

  • What made the Progressive-era reformers actually effective — what did they do that worked?
  • How can a movement from 130 years ago help you understand an issue in today's news? (the Now & Then thread, paying off all year)
  • What's the difference between civic action that makes change and civic action that just makes noise?
  • Who counts as "everyday people" — and what tools do they have that the powerful don't?

Signature project — the Civic Action Project

Student-facing overview

All semester, you've been keeping an Issue File on a national issue you chose — adding to it every week in our Now & Then routine, and looking at it through a different historical lens each unit. This unit, that file stops being homework and becomes ammunition. First we'll study the people who actually pulled it off: muckrakers who exposed what was hidden, suffragists who organized for seventy years, reformers who turned outrage into laws. Then you'll do what they did. You'll pick a lane from the choice board — write, speak, make, or do — and a tier that stretches you (Entry, Core, or Stretch), and turn your Issue File into a real Civic Action Product aimed at a real audience. Every path, every tier, one rubric: your Claim, your Evidence from the Issue File, your Action & Audience, and your Citations. Products are due June 22 — the last day of 8th grade. Go out making change.

Teacher notes: the year's capstone — the Now & Then routine and the "whose voice?" habit converge here. Rubric outcomes to attach in Canvas: ★ C4.6-8.5 (civic action on a national issue — the biggest civics gap this unit closes), ★ H4.6-8.3 (history informs today), and ★ SSS4.6-8.1 (thesis/product); C4.6-8.4, C2.6-8.4, H2.6-8.5, SSS3.6-8.1, and SSS4.6-8.2 ride on smaller tasks.
