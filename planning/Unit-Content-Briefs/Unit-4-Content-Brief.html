Unit 4 — A New Nation & Its Economy

← Course Overview · Outcomes Alignment · config.json draft
Weeks 20–23 · Jan 26–Feb 19, 2027 · Era: 1789–1840s · Theme color bronze #7E5A15 (Unit Theme System)

Driving question

What kind of nation — and economy — would the young United States become?

Supporting essential questions

  • When you're the first to do a job, how do your choices become rules for everyone who comes after? (precedent-setting)
  • When a young nation chases profit and growth, what values does it trade away — and who pays the price? (continues the profit-vs-values thread from Unit 1)
  • How did disagreements about money — debts, banks, and taxes — create America's first political parties?
  • Should a new, fragile nation stay out of the world's fights, or can it afford to? (early foreign policy & the War of 1812)

Signature project — “Founding an Economy”

Student-facing overview

It's 1790, and the United States is broke. You're in President Washington's cabinet, and Alexander Hamilton has just dropped a plan on the table that could save the country — or split it apart. Should the nation create a national bank? Take over the states' war debts? Put tariffs on imported goods? Tax whiskey, even if farmers on the frontier are ready to fight over it? You'll take on a cabinet role, dig into the evidence behind each option, and argue it out — because every choice has winners, losers, and a price tag. In the end, your cabinet issues its decisions and you defend them: what kind of economy should America have, and who should it work for?

Teacher notes: the simulation is the engine for the unit's economics push.
