Unit 5 — Expansion & Its Costs

← Course Overview · Outcomes Alignment · config.json draft
Weeks 24–27 · Feb 22–Mar 19, 2027 · Era: 1801–1850 · Theme color rust #8F3F1E (Unit Theme System)

Driving question

What drove the nation westward, and who paid the price?

Supporting essential questions

  • What can layered maps show us about expansion that a textbook paragraph can't — and how do maps explain why things happened where they did?
  • Was Manifest Destiny a genuine belief, a justification for taking land, or both — and does the difference matter?
  • What does sovereignty mean, and what happened when the United States recognized Native nations' sovereignty on paper and then violated it?
  • Why do people move — and who gets to move freely while others are moved by force? (migration thread that carries into Units 6 and 7)

Signature project — “The Layered Map”

Student-facing overview

A single map can hide as much as it shows — so you're going to build one that hides nothing. Layer by layer, you'll assemble a map of the United States from 1801 to 1850: territorial growth (the Louisiana Purchase, Texas, the Mexican Cession), migration routes (the Oregon Trail and the trails west), Indigenous nations (who was already on every acre of that “new” land), and resources (the farmland, furs, and gold that pulled people west). Then comes the real work: lay Manifest Destiny — the claim that all this was meant to be — over your finished map and weigh it against the people it displaced. Your final piece pairs your layered map with a perspectives study: the same stretch of land, explained through the eyes of the people who gained it and the people who paid for it. When the layers line up, patterns appear that no single map would ever admit to. Your job is to find them — and say what they mean.

Teacher notes: the map is the argument — students use geographic layers as evidence, not decoration. Rubric outcomes to attach in Canvas: ★ G1.6-8.6 (maps that explain dynamics), ★ G2.6-8.8 (migration/settlement patterns), and ★ H3.6-8.4 (perspectives); G1.6-8.6's map-construction work also serves G2.6-8.6 and G3.6-8.3, while H2.6-8.6 and H1.6-8.6 ride on smaller tasks.
