Unit 1 — Foundations & Identity

← Course Overview · Outcomes Alignment · Unit 1 Blueprint (classroom system) · config.json draft
Weeks 1–3 · Sep 2–18, 2026 · Era: pre-1763 · Theme color forest #1F4D3A (Unit Theme System)

Driving question

Who gets to define what an “American” is — and whose stories get told?

Supporting essential questions

  • What makes up a person's — or a nation's — identity, and who gets to decide?
  • How did geography shape three very different ways of life in the colonial regions?
  • What did contact mean for the peoples on each side of the Atlantic?
  • When people choose profit, what values do they trade away? (plants the economics seed for Unit 4)

Signature project — “Whose America?”

Student-facing overview

You'll start with your own story: a life map of the places, people, and moments that made you who you are. Then we'll zoom out and ask the same question about early America. You'll investigate the three colonial regions and the people whose stories often get left out — Indigenous nations who were already here, and enslaved Africans whose forced labor built colonial wealth — and decide for yourself: whose America was it? Your finished piece pairs your life map with a colonial perspective piece, told through a voice that isn't usually heard. Along the way, we'll also write our Classroom Constitution together — deciding whose voices count in this room.

Teacher notes: establishes the year's “whose voice?” habit (H3.6-8.4) and the Classroom Constitution. Rubric outcomes to attach in Canvas: ★ H3.6-8.4 (perspectives) and ★ G1.6-8.5 (regions); C4.6-8.4, E1.6-8.3, SSS3.6-8.1 ride on smaller tasks. See the alignment page.
