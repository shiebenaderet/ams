Teacher Guide

Now & Then

Weekly current events routine 8th Grade Social Studies About 10 minutes, once a week

One short bellringer each week. Students connect a piece of today's news to whatever unit you are studying. It builds a running record in the Bellringer Notebook that students draw on for the end of year civic action capstone, so the routine does double duty.

The weekly rhythm

  1. Now (3 min). Students read or watch a short news item. You can post one for the whole class, or let students bring their own once the routine is rolling.
  2. Source check (2 min). A quick SIFT pass (a four step habit for checking if a source can be trusted: Stop, Investigate the source, Find better coverage, Trace the claim back to where it started). Students mark whether the source seems trustworthy and why.
  3. Then (3 min). Students link the story to the unit. Every unit has a "Then anchor" on the last sheet of this packet to make the link easy.
  4. So what (2 min). One sentence: why this connection matters, or what it predicts.
Why it works

It hits the history to today standard every single week instead of once a year, keeps source skills warm, and quietly grows the evidence file students will turn into their civic action project. No new prep days. It lives inside the warm up you already run.

Standards it carries

  • H4.6-8.3 Analyze how a historical event helps us understand a current issue. (every week)
  • SSS1.6-8.1 / SSS2.6-8.2 Weigh evidence and judge whether a source is reliable.
  • SSS3.6-8.1 When you run a few of these as quick partner talks, students practice hearing more than one viewpoint.
  • C4.6-8.5 The running log feeds the year end civic action capstone, which is where this standard gets met.

Keeping it accessible

  • Use the Support version (next pages) for students who need sentence stems and a word bank.
  • Let students choose to write, draw, or talk their connection. A two panel "then and now" sketch counts.
  • Curate two or three trusted sources for the week so no one is lost in a search.
  • Keep a class running wall: "Stories we connected this year." It becomes a memory aid for the capstone.

"Then" Anchors by Unit

What today's news can connect back to

Each unit has a big idea that today's news keeps echoing. Point students at the anchor, then let them find the modern story (or you provide one). Sample current themes are just starting points.

Feeds the capstone

Tell students to star any entry tied to an issue they care about. At year's end they pick one starred thread and turn it into their civic action project. The research is already done, spread across the whole year.
