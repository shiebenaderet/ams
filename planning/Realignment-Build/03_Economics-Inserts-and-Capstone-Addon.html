Triangle Trade economics exit ticket

Run this right after the simulation reflection. Print as a half sheet.

Exit ticket
  • Supply and demand. Which goods were in high demand (lots of people wanted them) in the triangle trade? How did that shape what got traded?
  • Costs and benefits. Who gained wealth from this trade? Who paid the cost? Be specific.
  • Profit vs. people. The trade was profitable (it made money). Why is "it made money" not enough to call a choice good?

Support stems: People wanted ____________ the most. The people who gained were ____________. The people who paid the cost were ____________.

Unit 9 Insert

The Economy Lens, Named

Add to your North & South stations

You already study the North and the South through four lenses: Geography, Economy, Transportation, and Society. The Economy lens is doing real economics standards work. This insert just makes students name it.

At the Economy station, add these two questions
  • Resources and wealth (E4.6-8.5). How was wealth spread differently in the North and the South? Who held it, and what was it built on?
  • Supply and demand (E2.6-8.5). The South grew cotton the world wanted. How did that demand (how much people wanted it) shape the South's choices about labor and land?

Support stems: In the North, wealth came from ____________. In the South, wealth came from ____________. Because the world wanted cotton, the South ____________.

Then & Now

When one product drives a whole region's economy, the region depends on it. Can you name a place today whose economy leans on one thing?

Capstone Add-On

The Struggle Continues

Adds to "The Struggle for American Ideals"Civic action, fed by your Now & Then log

Your capstone already ends on a powerful idea: that excluded groups did not reject America's ideals, they demanded access to them. This add-on takes the next step. Students carry one ideal into today and take action on it. Because the Now & Then log gathered current stories all year, the heavy lifting is already done.

Question 3 (the new panel)

Pick one ideal you wrote about. Where is it still being fought for today? What will you do about it?
  • Find the issue in your Now & Then log (you starred it during the year).
  • Make a claim: what should happen, and why, using one example from history and one from today.
  • Take one real action from the menu below.
