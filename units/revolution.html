Unit 2: Revolution: Ideas & Voices

Weeks 6–11 · 1763–1783

What made independence worth fighting for — and for whom was it left incomplete?
  • How did loyal British subjects turn into revolutionaries in barely a decade?
  • The Declaration says “all men are created equal” — who was actually included, and who was left waiting?
  • Whose account of the Revolution survived, and whose did we have to go looking for?
Signature project: “Voices of the Revolution” podcast — teams research an overlooked participant in the Revolution and produce an episode telling that story from the sources.

Activities & Games

Causes of Unrest Stations activity analyzing the grievances that led to revolution. Taxes with no vote and no say pushed loyal colonists toward rebellion. Taxation without Representation Intolerable Acts Imagine being charged fees for things you have no say in — that’s how colonists felt paying British taxes they never got to vote on. Britain’s harsh punishment after the Boston Tea Party — closing a harbor and shutting down self-government — backfired and pushed the colonies together. Revolutionary Voices Hear from the people who lived through the Revolution. Not everyone chose the same side — and not everyone got remembered. Patriot & Loyalist Overlooked Voices Patriots wanted to break away from Britain and self-govern. Loyalists — about 1 in 5 colonists — chose to stay loyal to the king instead. The Revolution wasn’t only won by the famous names on money — poets, spies, and everyday people played big roles too, even when history skipped over them. Revolution Review Review the key ideas leading up to the Revolution. The Declaration promised rights — but who actually got them? Unalienable Rights Consent of the Governed Rights you’re born with, not gifts a king can take away. The Declaration names three: life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness. The idea that a government only has power because the people give it that power — not the other way around.
