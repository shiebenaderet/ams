Words you need

The same words at every level. The last three belong to one region each.

Cash crop
A plant grown to sell for money, not to eat. Academic: A crop grown to be sold for profit rather than eaten by the family that grew it. Tobacco, rice and indigo were cash crops.
Subsistence farming
Growing just enough food to feed your own family. Academic: Farming that produces enough to feed the household, with little or nothing left to sell. The opposite of growing a cash crop.
Indentured servant
Someone who worked for about 4 to 7 years to pay for the trip across the ocean. Then they were free. Academic: A person who signed a contract to work without wages for a set number of years, usually four to seven, in exchange for passage to America. At the end of the term they were free, and the contract had an end date.
Enslaved
Forced to work for no pay, with no right to leave. Enslaved people were treated as property that could be bought and sold. Academic: Held as property and forced to work without pay, for life, with that status passed to one’s children. Unlike indenture, it had no end date and no contract, and by law it was inherited.
Export
Something a place sells to another country. Academic: A good sent out of a colony to be sold somewhere else. What a region exports tells you what its land and labor could produce.
Self-government
People making their own local rules instead of taking orders from far away. Academic: A community making its own laws rather than receiving them from a distant authority. Every colony had some of it, and every colony also had a governor answering to Britain.
Puritan New England
A member of a religious group that wanted to live exactly by the Bible. Academic: An English Protestant who wanted to “purify” the Church of England. Puritans founded Massachusetts as a community run on their own religious rules.
Tolerance Middle
Letting people believe and worship differently from you. Academic: Allowing beliefs and practices you disagree with. In the Middle Colonies it was written into law, which is not the same as everyone getting along.
Plantation Southern
A very large farm that grew one crop to sell, worked by enslaved people. Academic: A large agricultural estate producing a single cash crop for export, worked by a concentrated labor force — by the 1700s, almost entirely enslaved Africans and their descendants.

New England Colonies

Massachusetts, Connecticut, Rhode Island, New Hampshire

The land and the weather

New England had four colonies: Massachusetts, Connecticut, Rhode Island, and New Hampshire. The soil there was thin and full of rocks. Glaciers (huge sheets of slow-moving ice) left those rocks behind long ago. The winters were long and cold, so the growing season was short.

Crops that need a lot of heat could not grow there. That is why New England had no .

But New England had two good things. It had deep harbors (sheltered water deep enough for ships to anchor safely). It had big forests full of tall trees.

How people made money

Farming could not make New Englanders rich. So they made money from the sea. They caught cod, a kind of fish. They dried it and salted it, and they sold it to other countries. Cod was New England’s biggest .

They cut down the tall trees and built ships. Boston became a busy port, where ships came in and went out.

New England also made rum. Ships brought molasses (thick dark syrup left over when sugar cane is boiled down) from the Caribbean, and New England turned it into rum. Traders took the rum to Africa and traded it for captured people. So New England money was tied to slavery too.

Most families farmed only to feed themselves. That is . It fed a family, but it did not make money.

Who settled here, and why

Most people came to New England for religion. They were . wanted to live by their own church rules. They came as whole families, so the towns grew fast.

Who made the rules

Britain sent a governor. But towns made many of their own rules at town meetings. Men who owned land voted on roads and schools. When people make their own rules, that is .

In 1620, the Pilgrims signed the Mayflower Compact. They promised to make “just and equal laws” together. Women, servants, and the Wampanoag people had no say. But the idea mattered later: a government should have the agreement of the people.

Middle Colonies

New York, Pennsylvania, New Jersey, Delaware

The land and the weather

The Middle Colonies were New York, Pennsylvania, New Jersey, and Delaware. They were in the middle, between New England and the South. The weather was in the middle too. Summers were warm and winters were cold. The growing season was long enough to grow a lot of food, and the soil was deep and good.

Two big rivers, the Hudson and the Delaware, ran to the ocean. Farmers put their grain on boats, and the rivers carried it to the ships.

How people made money

The Middle Colonies grew so much wheat that people called them the breadbasket (a region that grows most of the grain everyone else eats). Farmers ate some of their grain, and they sold the rest. So wheat was their food and their .

People also made things. They built ships and made iron tools. They cut trees into lumber at mills. Skilled workers made shoes and furniture by hand. New York City and Philadelphia became big ports.

Southern Colonies

Maryland, Virginia, North Carolina, South Carolina, Georgia

The land and the weather

The Southern Colonies were Maryland, Virginia, North Carolina, South Carolina, and Georgia. Summers were hot and winters were mild, so crops could grow for most of the year. The land was flat, and the soil was rich.

Wide rivers ran to the ocean. Ships sailed up the rivers right to the farms. So the South did not need many big port towns.

Who settled here, and why

Most people did not come to the South for religion. They came for land and money.

Georgia started in 1732. At first, Georgia did not allow slavery. Then planters asked to change that rule, and in 1751 slavery was allowed. This shows that slavery was a choice people made. The weather did not decide it.

Africans did not choose to come. They were captured, sold, and brought here by force.
