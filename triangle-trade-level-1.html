New England

Massachusetts · New Hampshire · Rhode Island · Connecticut · Port: Boston

Rules for New England

  • Rum rule: every 1 rum you trade away uses up 2 molasses from your port. Put the 2 molasses cards back in the teacher’s box.
  • You have 7 ships. More than you need: carry other regions’ cargo and charge a fee.
  • A ship carries 6 goods at most, each way. One voyage per Shipping round.

New England's soil is thin and rocky, so farms are small. The sea is where your wealth is. Your fishing boats catch far more fish than your people can eat, so you dry and salt the extra to sell. Whalers bring home whale oil for lamps. Your forests give lumber, and your shipyards build ships. You have 7 merchant ships, the most of any colony. Your main ports are Boston and Newport.

Rhode Island has distilleries (factories that make rum). Rum is made from molasses (the syrup left over from making sugar), and molasses comes from the West Indies. Game rule: each rum you trade uses up 2 molasses. The sugar islands pay well for fish and lumber. You are short of farm crops and of goods made in England. Two regions have no ships, so you can carry their cargo and charge for it. Try trading fish and lumber in the West Indies for molasses. Then your rum is ready to sell. Work out the best trades you can.

The molasses was made by enslaved Africans, people forced to work for life with no pay. Ships from Newport carried rum to West Africa and traded it for captured people. Then they carried those people across the Atlantic in chains. That forced crossing is called the Middle Passage.

Middle Colonies

New York · New Jersey · Pennsylvania · Delaware · Port: Philadelphia

Rules for Middle Colonies

  • You have 2 ships. Use them well, or carry other regions’ cargo for a fee.
  • A ship carries 6 goods at most, each way. One voyage per Shipping round.

Farms in the Middle Colonies grow huge amounts of wheat and corn, far more than your people can eat. The region was called the breadbasket of the colonies. You also raise cattle and cut lumber from your forests. Traders get furs from Native Americans, and furs bring a high price in England. Your two main ports are Philadelphia and New York City. You have 2 merchant ships. That is enough to trade on your own, but not to carry a lot.

Every region needs food, and you grow it. But wheat and cattle are heavy and cheap. You are short of manufactured goods (things made by skilled workers in workshops) and of goods from warm places. Sugar is expensive in England. If you trade wheat in the West Indies for sugar, you can take the sugar to England and trade it for the manufactured goods you lack. Try to make the best trades you can.

The sugar islands grew almost no food, because nearly every field was sugar cane. Your wheat, cattle, and lumber fed and built the plantations. There, enslaved Africans were forced to work for no pay. They had been carried across the Atlantic in chained ships. That forced crossing is called the Middle Passage. Slavery was legal in the Middle Colonies too. New York City had one of the largest numbers of enslaved people of any colonial city.

Southern Colonies

Maryland · Virginia · N. Carolina · S. Carolina · Georgia · Port: Charleston

Rules for Southern Colonies

  • You have no ships. To send goods, hire another region’s ship (pay the fee in goods, carried as cargo) or trade for a ship card.
  • In Negotiation you can’t send your Merchant out. Other regions’ Merchants come to you, so be ready to make deals at your port.
  • A ship carries 6 goods at most, each way. One voyage per Shipping round.

Tobacco is the great cash crop (a crop grown to sell) of the Southern Colonies. You grow so much that the price has dropped, and England pays little for it. South Carolina grows rice, some silk, and indigo (a plant that makes a blue dye). North Carolina makes naval stores (pine tar and resin used to build ships). You also have fish, lumber, and furs. That is eight kinds of goods, the most of any region. Your main ports are Charleston and Savannah. But you have no ships.

England's cloth makers want your indigo, and English buyers want your tobacco and rice. Shipbuilders everywhere want naval stores. You are short of food grains and of manufactured goods (things made by skilled workers). Your first problem is moving your goods. Hire a ship from New England, the Middle Colonies, or England and pay with some of your goods, or trade for a ship of your own. Then work out the best trades you can.

Your crops were planted and harvested by enslaved Africans and their children, who were forced to work for life with no pay. In South Carolina, more than half of all the people were enslaved. Many had been carried from West Africa across the Atlantic in crowded ships. That forced crossing is called the Middle Passage.

West Indies

Jamaica · Barbados · Antigua · St. Kitts · Port: Kingston

Rules for West Indies

  • You have no ships. To send goods, hire another region’s ship (pay the fee in goods, carried as cargo) or trade for a ship card.
  • In Negotiation you can’t send your Merchant out. Other regions’ Merchants come to you, so be ready to make deals at your port.
  • A ship carries 6 goods at most, each way. One voyage per Shipping round.

The islands of the West Indies, such as Jamaica, Barbados, Antigua, and St. Kitts, are covered with sugar cane. Sugar is expensive and wanted all over the world, so you hold the most valuable good in the game. Making sugar also leaves molasses (a dark syrup), which New England turns into rum. You also grow citrus fruit, like limes and oranges. Your main port is Kingston, in Jamaica. You have no ships.

England pays high prices for sugar, and New England wants your molasses for its rum. But planters used nearly every field for cane, so the islands grow little else. You are short of food and of manufactured goods (things made by skilled workers). Hire a ship from New England, the Middle Colonies, or England to carry your cargo, or trade for a ship. Sugar is your strongest card, so do not give it away cheaply. Work out the best trades you can.

The sugar was made by enslaved Africans, who were forced to plant, cut, and boil the cane in heat and danger, with no pay. Most people on the islands were enslaved. They had been taken from Africa and carried across the Atlantic in chained, crowded ships. That forced crossing is called the Middle Passage.

England

The Mother Country · Port: London

Rules for England

  • Duties: when a chance card makes another region pay a duty (a tax), the goods go to you.
  • You have 8 ships. More than you need: carry other regions’ cargo and charge a fee.
  • A ship carries 6 goods at most, each way. One voyage per Shipping round.

You represent the merchants of England, the Mother Country (the nation that rules the colonies). English workshops make manufactured goods (things made by skilled workers): guns, gunpowder, tools, books, clothing, and fine furniture. You also hold silver. Your main port is London. You have 8 merchant ships, the most in the game.

England believed colonies existed to make it rich. That idea is called mercantilism. The colonies had to buy manufactured goods from England. They had to sell many of their crops to England too. But England lacks the goods of colonial farms and forests. You must import a long list of colonial goods. Trade your manufactured goods for colonial raw materials (goods from nature, like crops and wood). Two regions have no ships, so you can also carry their cargo and charge for it. Work out the best trades you can.

English ships also carried people. They took cloth, guns, and metal goods to West Africa and traded them for captured Africans. Then they carried those people across the Atlantic in chains. That forced crossing is called the Middle Passage. In the Americas, the people who lived were sold into slavery. The ships came home with sugar and tobacco grown by enslaved people.
